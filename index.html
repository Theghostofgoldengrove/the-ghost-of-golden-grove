Roman Ruins

A 5-Part Post-Apocalyptic Short Story • 1,790 Words

Chapter 1

In the year 2138, the world that remained was a quiet ruin of overgrown lawns and sagging rooftops. Maple Grove had once been an ordinary suburban town. Now its streets cracked under weeds, its houses leaned like tired old men, and the only law left was the law of the clans. Survivors had rebuilt society the only way they still remembered—high-school tribes. Nerds hoarded what little knowledge survived. Band geeks turned broken instruments into signals and noise. Jocks claimed the open fields and the old football stadium, ruling by muscle and steel. Trust was rare. Everyone carried weapons forged in the style of ancient Rome: short swords, spears, heavy shields hammered from scavenged metal. Guns were a forgotten myth, at least as far as anyone still living could prove.

Alex Rivera was nineteen and belonged to none of them. He had grown up far from any town, on a hard scrap of country land where the only teacher was necessity. He knew how to track, how to listen to the wind, how to make a meal from almost nothing, and how to stay invisible when larger, louder people decided the world belonged to them. That country childhood had kept him alive after the Scourge. It had also left him odd in the eyes of the survivors—nerdy in the way he studied old diagrams and quiet patterns, yet athletic enough to move like a hunter when the moment demanded it. He carried a Roman gladius at his hip and a light shield on his back. Across his shoulders rode something of his own invention: a makeshift crossbow built from scavenged wood, steel springs, and tensioned cord. It launched kitchen knives with enough force to punch through scrap armor at close range. He never showed it unless he had no other choice.

On a gray afternoon he moved through an abandoned house the way he always did—slow, methodical, checking every shadow. He found a sealed jar of pickled vegetables that had somehow survived the decades and slipped it into his pack. Outside, shouting rose. He peered through a gap in the boards. A group of Jocks in patched football pads and dented breastplates had cornered two Nerds against a chain-link fence. Their leader, Brad "Titan" Voss, stood half a head taller than the rest. He carried a heavy Roman-style spatha and a rectangular shield reinforced with car-hood steel. One of the Nerds, a thin man with cracked glasses, raised empty hands. "We were just looking for batteries," the Nerd said. "We weren't—"

Titan's spatha came down in a brutal, unnecessary arc. The blade sheared through the man's raised forearm, severing it cleanly at the elbow. The Nerd screamed as arterial blood sprayed across the cracked asphalt. Titan did not stop. He drove the point of the sword into the man's gut, twisted, and ripped upward, opening him from navel to sternum. Intestines spilled in a wet coil. The Nerd collapsed, still twitching, trying to hold his own insides in with the stump of his arm.

The second Nerd tried to run. Titan's men tripped him with a spear shaft. Titan stepped on the back of the man's neck, pinning him face-down, then raised the heavy spatha high and brought it down again and again—hacking through spine, ribs, and finally the skull with wet, deliberate cracks until the body stopped moving and the head was little more than red pulp. Titan wiped the blade on the dead man's shirt, breathing hard from the effort, and smiled.

"You read books," he said to the corpses. "Books don't feed us. Hands do. And now neither of you has either." Alex watched only long enough to be sure the path was clear, then left by the back window. He did not interfere. Interference got people killed, and he had learned long ago that the country taught a simple rule: survive first.

Chapter 2

That evening he sat alone on a rusted jungle gym in the overgrown park, eating the cold pickles and sketching in a small notebook—water sources, weak points in armor, the angles of Roman formations he had taught himself from a single surviving book. A soft cough broke the quiet.

Jordan Hale stood at the edge of the trees. Eighteen, pale under a hood, a light spear in one hand. A faint dark rash traced the side of her neck. She did not raise the weapon. "You're the one they call the Ghost," she said. "No clan." Alex closed the notebook. "And you're the one who's supposed to be gone by now."

Jordan almost smiled. She sat at a careful distance. "Not yet. The Scourge takes its time with some of us. It isn't contagious anymore—hasn't been for years. But it's still killing me. Faster every week." Before Alex could answer, a smaller figure burst from the undergrowth. Sam Hale was fourteen, wild-eyed, filthy, clutching a sharpened length of rebar like a Roman pilum. Sam snarled at Alex and planted both feet between him and Jordan, ready to kill.

"Sam. Stop," Jordan said quietly. Sam did not lower the weapon, but the murder in those eyes softened only for Jordan. Protective. Homicidal to everything else. "Younger sibling?" Alex asked. "The only family left," Jordan answered. "Don't make a sudden move. Sam doesn't do sudden moves well." Alex stayed still. After a long moment Sam retreated a few steps but never fully turned away.
